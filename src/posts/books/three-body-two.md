I finished reading *The Dark Forest* (Book 2 of the Three-Body trilogy) on 28 October 2026.

This book is an absolute masterpiece. Even though I already knew parts of the plot from listening to Lao Gao videos years ago, knowing the general story line didn't ruin a single second of the experience. The introduction and execution of Luo Ji (邏輯) is as impressive as it gets.

The imagery Cixin Liu builds is unreal—visualizing how a single, pristine **Water Droplet** travels through thousands of human warships, punching through hull after hull, turning the entire fleet into a burning, red hell in space. The human reactions throughout the book feel so naive and dumb, yet so painfully accurate to how society actually operates when faced with extinction. Science, technology, sociology, and art are all woven together seamlessly.

I tried not to highlight too many passages on my Kobo because individual lines lose their weight out of context, but a few quotes and scenes stood out.

I finished *The Dark Forest* on 28 October 2026. 

This book is an absolute masterpiece. No wonder people praise it endlessly. Even though I already knew parts of the storyline beforehand (thanks to Lao Gao videos) and knew about Luo Ji (邏輯) and his Wallfacer plan, reading the actual text didn't make the experience any weaker. If anything, it made it infinitely heavier.

The writing, the imagery, the scale—it handles technology, humanity, history, and art all in one breath. The sheer visual horror of the **Water Droplet** slicing through thousands of human space warships, turning humanity's proudest fleet into a fiery graveyard of red and burning hell, is unforgettable. Human society in this book reacts in ways that are so naive, so dumb, and yet completely realistic. It’s scary because *that is us*.

Here are some of the lines and themes that stuck with me the most.

---

## 1. Love as an Idea vs. Reality

> *"不是的，大部分人的愛情對象也只是存在於自己的想像之中... 他們遲早會發現夢中情人與模板之間的差異... 如果無法適應就分開..."*

This section blew me away. It argues that when people fall in love, they aren't actually loving the real physical person in front of them—they are falling for their own internal illusion, a perfect mental template. 

It made me realize that love often isn't about the physical reality, but about the *idea* of someone, the construct that saves your personal world.

> *"邏輯知道，自己心靈中最僻靜的疆土已經屬於她了，她將在那裡伴隨他的一生。外面那個太陽就永遠隱藏在陰雨和夜色中吧，這個世界只要有火光和她就夠了。"*

When you read Luo Ji's devotion, it poses a quiet question: do we choose to stay in our crafted, perfect world of illusion, or do we force ourselves into the cold truth? 

---

## 2. Leaving the Calm Waters into the Muddy Sea

> *"老師，我挺難的，都說畢業後走向社會，就像魚兒游進了大海，可大海很渾，我什麼都看不清，總想游到一處清清的海，游得好累。"*

As someone graduating soon, this quote felt uncomfortably vivid and relatable. Everyone tells you that entering society is like a fish entering the vast ocean. But the reality is that the ocean is muddy, visibility is zero, and you spend all your energy just swimming, desperately searching for a patch of clear water. 

---

## 3. Dark Forest Deterrence & The Hot Air Balloon Paradox

> *"即使逃亡成功，那麼倖存下來的也不再是人類文明，而是另一種黑暗邪惡的東西。"*

To survive in a cold, resource-scarce universe, living beings are forced to strip away their humanity, love, and morals. Survival requires absolute calculation.

This leads to some of the most haunting scenes in the entire book. Like the starship accelerating under ultra-high G-forces, crushing crew members flat against the walls until only blood remains (which then gets separated and diluted as sediments drift to the back). Or the Mexican standoff in deep space, where ships are seconds away from preemptively murdering each other for resources to survive.

It brings back those classic moral dilemma debates from primary school—like the "Hot Air Balloon" debate where someone has to be thrown overboard so the rest can survive:

* Do you throw out the doctor, the farmer, the elder, or the child?
* Is it better to make a cold, logical decision, or refuse to choose at all?

*The Dark Forest* shows that in extreme survival scenarios, **the worst decision is not making one**. Making the logical choice feels suffocating, but waiting for someone else to step up and make it for you is just human laziness.

---

## Final Verdict: 1/3

I give this book a **1/3**—simply because it's book 2 of 3 in the trilogy! 

Is it a full-score book? I can only answer that once I've read every book in human history. But for now, this story, the horror of the Droplet, and the dark silence of the universe will stay rent-free in my brain for the next two decades.

Until next time! 👋
