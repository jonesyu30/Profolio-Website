I finished Austin Kleon’s *Show Your Work!* on 15 July 2026.

This is a remarkably quick, effortless read. In fact, it almost feels *too* easy at times—like scrolling through a really well-crafted YouTube video or a visual thread. But it does its job: it keeps you hooked from cover to cover and gives you a practical, no-nonsense kick to stop hoarding your ideas and start putting yourself out there. 

Even on an e-reader screen in plain black-and-white, the layout, typography, and illustrations look fantastic. And what makes the book actually work is that Kleon practices what he preaches: by the end of the book, he shows his own messy process, proving that these simple, bite-sized strategies actually function in the real world. No "mindfulness" fluff, no pushy marketing speak—just consecutive points and sharp logic.

---

## 1. Hoarding vs. Generosity (The Logic of Emptying Your Cup)

Right near the beginning, Kleon drops a counterintuitive piece of advice: start reading obituaries every morning to remind yourself of mortality. When I tried it, reading about retired American baseball players didn't feel super relatable—I might need to find a localized Hong Kong equivalent—but the underlying logic hit home when he talks about hoarding your ideas:

> *"The problem with hoarding is you end up living off your reserves. Eventually, you'll become stale... Somehow the more you give away, the more comes back to you."*

It sounds like superstitious karma at first, but logically it makes total sense. If you keep all your ideas to yourself, you stay comfortable and stagnant. When you share everything you know, you empty your cup, forcing yourself to stay curious, observe the world, and learn new things to replenish your reserves.

## 2. Loving Your "Garbage" & The Power of Story

> *"You have to have the courage to keep loving your garbage."*

This line inspired me to start my own "garbage list"—a collection of weird, niche, or uncool things I genuinely love, regardless of whether anyone else hates them. That list is your actual personality, your stance, and nobody can take it away from you.

Kleon also brings in psychology professor Paul Bloom to explain why packaging and context matter so much:

> *"You might think that the pleasure you get from painting depends on its color and its shape... It shouldn't matter whether it's an original or a forgery."*

Visually, a perfect forgery and a Da Vinci are identical. But the original is worth millions because of the **story** behind it. People don't just consume content; they consume the human story behind the creator.

## 3. Chase Hearts, Not Algorithms

One section felt uncomfortably personal:

> *"Don't waste your time reading articles about how to get more followers. Don't waste time following people online just because you think it'll get you somewhere."*

This hits the exact problem with platforms like Reels, Shorts, or Threads. People scroll past, drop a mindless like, and forget who you are three seconds later. There’s zero personality retention. Chasing algorithm hacks just gives you ghost followers, whereas building a genuine voice—one where you aren't afraid to share raw, un-sugarcoated work like George Orwell suggested—builds real human connection.

---

## Storytelling Templates & Chain Projects

Kleon shares a simple Pixar-style storytelling framework that instantly gave me an urge to go edit a video:

> *"Once upon a time, there was ____. Every day, ____. One day, ____. Because of that, ____. Because of that, ____. Until finally, ____."*

It’s such a simple, powerful structure to turn chaotic thoughts into a coherent narrative.

He also introduces the concept of **chain projects**—using the momentum at the end of one project to light up the fuse for the next. When you keep yourself moving in a continuous chain, procrastination loses its grip because you never stop long enough to get paralyzed by overthinking.

---

## The Takeaway

I’ll leave myself with two quotes to keep in mind for the future:

> *"Anyone who isn't embarrassed of who they were last year probably isn't learning enough."*

We should constantly be learning, adapting, and leveling up. Hopefully, one day, we can look back at where we started and be amazed by how far we've come.

If you’re stuck in a creative rut, terrified of putting your unpolished work online, or overthinking your next step, pick this up. It takes an hour to read, but the mindset shift lasts a lot longer.
