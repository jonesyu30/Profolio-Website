Hi guys, I'm Jones. Welcome to my blog! 

I'm definitely not someone who reads all the time—I’m just a casual reader and have only finished a handful of books in my life. However, the books I *do* actually finish tend to have a massive impact on me. If you’re also a casual reader looking to try something new, this is a great place to start.

*Psycho-Cybernetics* was the first large book I actually managed to finish. What an accomplishment! I’ve skimmed through university library books on philosophy before, but I never quite finished any of them. After seeing this one recommended by a YouTuber, I gave it a shot.

It did not disappoint. The book focuses on self-image and practical strategies to handle difficult, high-stress situations. Since feeling down or overwhelmed is inevitable, having a mental framework to cope with it is essential.

---

## The Mental "Safe Room"

For me, the most memorable concept in the book is the **mental safe room**—a place in your mind where you can be 100% relaxed, completely free from negative emotions. Imagine a perfect room with the most comfortable chair and a serene view, where every bad thought simply vanishes.

It might sound unrealistic, or like something only rich people who can afford custom dream homes can enjoy. But that’s the beauty of it: everyone can access their own safe room at any time because it lives entirely inside your mind.

Some might call this escapism since it doesn't solve real-world problems directly. But think of it this way: **you need a calm mind to think clearly.** 

For me, this tool is invaluable when bad events happen. Even if a situation doesn't hurt me physically, it can inflict a heavy mental toll—usually when other people's actions make me angry. Trying to fix or change other people is rarely worth the time. By controlling my own internal state instead, I can at least preserve my peace.
