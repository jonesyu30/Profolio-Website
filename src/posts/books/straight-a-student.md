I picked up *How to Become a Straight-A Student* during my second year of university. The book breaks down concrete strategies for prepping for exams, taking notes, and writing academic papers. 

Full disclosure: I didn't finish the entire book. I skipped the second half because it heavily focuses on essay writing for liberal arts subjects. Since my Computer Science major currently consists almost entirely of heavy math courses, the paper-writing advice wasn't as relevant to my workload. But if you’re in a major that requires writing constant essays, those chapters will be a goldmine.

---

## Studying Doesn't Have to Be Painful

Beyond the practical study hacks, the most eye-opening takeaway is that **studying shouldn't feel like endless, tedious punishment.**

Coming from Hong Kong and going through the HKDSE system, the cultural norm was to study for 8+ hours a day by sheer willpower. Whether or not it was efficient, staring at notes for hours on end was just what everyone did. 

Newport challenges this head-on: based on interviews with straight-A students worldwide, high-level academic performance comes from **short, intense, highly focused sessions**, not slow, painful hours spent staring passively at textbooks.

The book covers everything from note-taking systems and exam prep to real-world case studies of students adopting these exact methods. If you're feeling overwhelmed or anxious in college, this is definitely worth a read.
