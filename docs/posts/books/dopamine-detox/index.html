Dopamine Detox

By Thibaut Meurisse

I finished reading Dopamine Detox by Thibaut Meurisse on 15 September 2026.

School just started, and to be blunt, I feel completely overwhelmed. There’s this heavy, suffocating feeling—like a dark smoke called 一事無成 (accomplishing nothing) floating around me. It’s tasteless and odorless, but constantly pressing down.

This book didn’t fix that overnight. It felt like getting a single XP orb in Hypixel Skyblock: a tiny, temporary boost that almost doesn't matter two seconds later. Maybe I’ve already read too much about productivity and self-help to find huge revelations here. But even if the boost is small, I don't need a total life overhaul right now—I just need enough clarity to get through the next day, and then figure out the day after that.

Here are the key takeaways and quotes I highlighted while trying to parse through the noise.

1. Long-Term Vision vs. Over-Stimulation

"Studies have shown that one of the best predictors of success is the ability to think long term."

Overcoming procrastination is really just an ongoing battle between long-term intention and short-term distraction. When I catch myself stalling, my plan is to stop and reconnect with the original motive for a few minutes: Why am I doing this? What future—what job, internship, or better tomorrow—does this action actually build?

At the same time, the book points out a root cause for why staying focused is so hard:

"While there are many reasons to procrastinate, the most important one — and the one overlooked most often — is overstimulation."

Every morning, the moment I wake up: BAM! Immediate stimulation. If constant inputs lead directly to procrastination and that lingering guilt that follows, cutting down the baseline noise is necessary just to function.

2. A Sustainable Detox (One Thing at a Time)

"No, watching motivational videos all day long won’t help you reach your goals. But performing daily consistent actions, sustained over a long period of time will."

Reading this book won't reach my goals for me either. The author outlines different detox protocols, including a full 48-hour complete shutdown. Honestly, a full 2-day drastic detox feels tedious for a temporary reset.

Instead, I’m opting for a sustainable approach: detoxing one specific high-dopamine habit at a time. Focus on the single front in front of you, but make it a permanent shift rather than a weekend stunt.

3. Grounding Questions to Stop the Spiral

The most striking moments in the book were the questions designed to interrupt mental spirals:

"And I almost never worry about missing specific events because I know there will always be more events to enjoy in the future."

If you are already filling your time with meaningful work or genuine rest, you aren't actually missing out on anything. You made the best decision for your time, and the world goes on.

"What goals are you pursuing? Are they the right ones for you? Are you making progress toward them each day? And if you keep doing what you are doing, will you reach them?"

A necessary check to ensure I’m not subconsciously self-sabotaging or running on autopilot.

"If I could complete only one task today before taking a month off, what would that task be?"

This forced me to pause. Usually, finishing one task just opens the door to ten more tasks, or leaves an empty void that I immediately fill with more screen stimulation. Narrowing it down to one critical item cuts through that paralysis.

Stop Living in the Next Task

"Often, people become stuck in their head thinking of their next task or worrying about problems in their life. Don’t be like them."

This hit hard. I am constantly trapped in my own head, obsessing over the next item, the next deadline, and the next item on the to-do list. The irony is that by worrying about everything coming up, I get overwhelmed and end up doing nothing.

You can either protect your focus by building intentional habits and systems, or remain unprepared and let every distraction hijack your time. I don't need a massive, perfect transformation today—just small, sustained changes to keep the smoke at bay.
