    I finally finished The Three-Body Problem (Book 1).

    It is officially the longest book I’ve ever finished in my life, and completing it gives me a massive sense of accomplishment. At the same time, realizing this is barely one-third of the entire trilogy makes me feel a bit behind. I’m going to take a break here and explore a few other books before diving into Books 2 and 3, but I’m definitely coming back for the aliens later.

    Also, a quick shoutout to my e-reader: there is zero chance I would have finished a book this dense if I was holding a physical copy. It turned out to be one of the best investments I've made for building a reading habit.

    Why You Can’t Just Watch a Summary Video

    I’ve known about this book for years. As a kid, it felt like this mythical container of advanced sci-fi concepts that my brain could never grasp. Years ago, I watched the famous Lao Gao (老高) video about it, then the Netflix adaptation, and about a third of the Tencent series. I already knew the entire plot.

    So why read it?

    Because you can never truly get the essence of a book from a third-party source.

    Explainer videos, AI summaries, and quotes miss the atmospheric dread and the pacing that make the knowledge actually sink in. You have to force yourself to sit down and read the text to feel it.

    1. The Historical Background Hit the Hardest

    The sci-fi physics in Book 1 are surprisingly coherent and intelligible, but the part that caught me off guard was the opening during the Cultural Revolution.

    Reading those first few chapters made me go down a rabbit hole of historical documentaries. It was wild to realize how much history I’ve missed. The killings, the ideological fervor (熱血)—it describes a world created by modern humans that feels so close, yet completely alien. It’s a reality that hopefully will never be recreated in our lifetime, and reading it gives you the exact context you need to understand Ye Wenjie’s choice to lose faith in humanity.

    2. Big Brain Moves and Apocalypse Energy

    Throughout the whole story, there is an underlying sense that the world is ending, yet everything unfolds with logical precision.

    My favorite parts were the "big brain" plays from smart characters. Take Shi Qiang (Da Shi)—he’s just a rough-around-the-edges cop, yet he connects complex dots using pure observation and practical data linkage when the world's elite scientists are losing their minds.

    Chinese Prose at Its Finest: Highlights

    One thing that blew me away was how Cixin Liu uses the Chinese language. It’s not about using rare vocabulary or forced rhymes; it’s about simple imagery that delivers a heavy emotional punch.

    "那沒有哭出和喊出的東西在她的血液中瀰漫、溶解、將陪伴她一生。"
    (The things she didn't cry or scream out diffused and dissolved in her blood, destined to accompany her for the rest of her life.)

    She didn't shed a tear, yet this single sentence tells you everything about her trauma. It doesn't show on the outside—it’s entirely internal, running through her veins.

    "她像一顆星星，總是那麼遙遠，照到我身上的光也總是冷的。"
    (She is like a star—always so distant, and the light that reaches me is always cold.)

    This describes a partnership built on scientific work rather than love. Normally, comparing someone to a star is a compliment—a warm light guiding you in the dark. But here, it’s flipped to highlight the physical reality of a star: millions of light-years away, casting light that is completely cold when it reaches you. Beautiful and heartbreaking.

    "這是，在一萬二千兆赫波段上，太陽是銀河系中最亮的一顆星。"
    (At the 12,000 MHz band, the sun was the brightest star in the entire Milky Way.)

    When the radio message is amplified through the sun, it’s a brilliant reminder of our limited human perspective. To our eyes, the sun is just a ball of light. But on another frequency, it's a deafening cosmic signal. It shows how much is happening in the universe right in front of us that our biological senses completely miss.

    The Verdict

    It’s not a "perfect" standalone story because it’s clearly setting up the massive interstellar war for the next two books, but in terms of worldbuilding, coherence, and writing quality, it lived up to all the hype. I’m taking a breather for now, but I’m ready for the next phase when the time comes.
